# Cybersecurity Policy and Incident Analysis

**Subject Area:** Information Security Policy · Incident Analysis · Risk Management · Governance

---

## What It Does

This collection of policy and analysis documents examines cybersecurity through a governance and risk management lens. The work spans breach forensics, ransomware threat analysis, and the evolving role of the BISO (Business Information Security Officer) — demonstrating the ability to translate technical security concepts into organizational policy, executive communication, and strategic risk framing.

### Documents Included

| File | Description |
|------|-------------|
| `Marriott_Cybersecurity_Breach_Analysis.docx` | In-depth analysis of the 2018 Marriott/Starwood data breach: attack timeline, root cause analysis, regulatory response (GDPR fine), organizational failures, and post-incident remediation recommendations |
| `Ransomware_Essay.docx` | Analysis of the ransomware threat landscape: attack economics, encryption mechanisms, threat actor profiles, ransom payment considerations, and organizational defense recommendations |
| `The_Business_Information_Security_Officer.docx` | Examination of the BISO role: how security leadership should position itself relative to business units, risk appetite translation, and the organizational structure of modern security programs |

## Problem Addressed

Technical security controls fail when they are disconnected from organizational context, business risk appetite, and regulatory requirements. This work addresses the governance layer: what went wrong in one of the largest hotel chain breaches in history, how ransomware economics create incentive structures that shape attacker behavior and victim decision-making, and how security leadership can be structured to ensure technical recommendations survive contact with business reality.

## Practical Relevance

Security engineers who understand policy, governance, and risk framing are significantly more effective in enterprise environments. This is especially true in:

- Federal contracting and compliance-driven environments (RMF, NIST, FISMA) where security decisions require documented rationale and executive buy-in
- Incident response: understanding the breach lifecycle and regulatory reporting obligations
- Security leadership roles: translating security requirements into business risk language
- GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance) programs building organizational security policy from scratch

## Tools, Languages, and Libraries

Policy research methodology · NIST CSF · GDPR/regulatory analysis · incident response frameworks · technical writing

## Skills Demonstrated

- Breach analysis: reconstructing attack timelines and identifying root causes from public reporting and regulatory filings
- Threat analysis: framing ransomware as a business problem with economic incentives — not just a technical one
- Governance: analyzing security leadership structures and their organizational effectiveness
- Regulatory fluency: interpreting GDPR enforcement actions and mapping them to organizational controls
- Executive communication: writing security analysis for non-technical and mixed audiences
